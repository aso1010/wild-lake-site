Argelys Samuel Oriach is a Dominican-born actor for stage and screen, writer, vocalist, and songwriter. His practice brings together performance, music, and the written word, exploring character and narrative through acting, vocals, songwriting, playwriting, and literary fiction.

He is the author of the digital short This Is Not a Love Story and is currently writing a play and his debut autofiction novel, Graduation.

Jordy
Oriach.

Left-handed power. An unconventional path from the Dominican Republic through junior college, New Mexico, and the University of Georgia. Built through persistence and progression.

The path & the player ↓
The athlete

Built
differently.

From Haverhill, Massachusetts, to two years of high school in the Dominican Republic, Jordy Oriach's route into Division I baseball was anything but linear. Frank Phillips College gave him an opening. At New Mexico, his game accelerated: he led the Mountain West in hits, total bases, doubles, runs, on-base percentage, and slugging percentage in 2025.

He went on to play for Georgia in 2026. The defining throughline is not a straight ascent—it is the capacity to adapt, develop, and keep going.

We read Oriach’s practice through humility, persistence, and hope. However impressive the record on paper, artistry lives in sustained attention to craft: returning to the work, remaining teachable, and refining the details of play. Recognition from the external world—including MLB—can open a door, but it cannot replace that attention or determine the work’s worth. We see hope sustaining the practice through uncertainty, with persistence giving it daily form.

Visit Sophia’s website
The writer

Story.
Across forms.

Sophia Jennings is a Los Angeles–based writer and filmmaker. She works across film, interviews, and advertising, with story at the center.

She is Director of Story at MakeMake. Her films have screened internationally, and her interviews appear in Harper’s BAZAAR, Rolling Stone, and ESQUIRE.

Born in San Francisco, educated in London and at Wesleyan. A practice that moves between forms without losing its own direction.

We understand Jennings’s writing as a form of self-love: the sustained attention required to give one’s experience language, shape, and consequence. We see the discipline of drafting and revision as a way of taking one’s perceptions seriously, while allowing them to change. The work gives that care a form that can be shared with others.

The writer

At the table.
In the story.

Emily Wilson writes about food and the people behind it. Her work spans features, profiles, guides, and odes.

She is the author of The Angel, an IACP Award-winning newsletter exploring food, restaurants, and the people who shape how we eat.

We approach The Angel as a site of editorial power: a newsletter that directs attention toward particular figures and brands in the food industry, helping shape who becomes visible and where cultural and commercial value accumulates. We ask what the title’s suggestion of a benevolent messenger does to frame that authority—and how taste, access, capitalism, and whiteness inform whose work receives its attention.

Zachary Reed
Kantor.

New York City–based design director and musician. Working across product, branding, graphic, and motion design, he leads design at GIPHY.

Visit Zachary’s website
The designer & musician

Visual clarity.
Cultural resonance.

Zachary Reed Kantor specializes in UI/UX, branding, and the interplay between them. His practice brings together product design, hand-drawn typography, illustration, and motion.

At GIPHY, he has designed the app, website, and experiences across the platform. He previously helped create Tapslash, an iPhone keyboard acquired by GIPHY in 2018 that became its SDK.

A musician who has toured in multiple bands, he also works with musicians and artists on album artwork, show posters, and vinyl packaging. He created Treble.fm to help music makers find collaborators.

We interpret Kantor’s practice as an interrogation of artistic posturing and self-fashioning: how stance, gesture, movement, and presentation make mastery legible. Across music and design, the body becomes part of the work’s visual language. We ask how practiced bodily mechanics become an image of artistic command—and how that image can carry the discipline, labor, and technique behind it. We also read this self-fashioning through art as bohemia: the cultivation of creative freedom, informality, and distance from convention. That image gains weight when considered alongside the money, privilege, and material security that can sustain it. We consider how the performance of freedom makes those conditions visible or allows them to recede.

Animation, sculpture & installation

Jessica
Wilson.

Multidisciplinary artist exploring how virtual systems register in bodies, objects, and everyday infrastructure. Born in New York City, she lives in Amsterdam and NYC.

Jessica at diez gallery
The artist

Unseen forces.
Physical sensation.

Jessica Wilson works across computer-generated animation, sculpture, and installation. Through light, color, and spatial relationships, she makes the bodily and material effects of virtual systems tangible.

Her practice gives everyday infrastructure an imagined inner life. Anthropomorphized electrical outlets and repurposed New York City taxi tops become surrogates for anxiety, neurosis, and human inexactitude.

We read Wilson’s work as exposing the psychological effects of capitalism and whiteness from within the privileged conditions they produce. We interpret this to be an examination of how synthetic infrastructure imitates, displaces, and reorganizes the living world, reshaping our sensory and emotional relationships with it.

We also see Wilson’s work as an exploration of the mechanics and manipulation of attention: how an inner state becomes a physical encounter that directs what others notice and feel. Money, space, and institutional backing help determine whose interior life acquires that reach. Siren prompts us to ask who has the means to build infrastructure—and whose inner world becomes part of the world others inhabit.
