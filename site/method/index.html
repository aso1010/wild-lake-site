Meet the founder

The structural principle

Nature is brutalist.

Nothing in it is decorative, and everything carries weight. We build infrastructure for artists under that same principle.

Clients come to Wild-Lake when the way they work has become unsustainable. The engagement is a controlled demolition, undertaken with the artist. We take the practice down to its studs, dismantling habits and brittle systems that can no longer carry the work. Then we rebuild around what the artist needs to operate with clarity.

We dismantle the architecture of extraction. The artist survives the loop.

Critique & revision

At Wild-Lake, development requires close study of the material and aggressive revision with the artist. Critique is the primary mechanism of care. It demands examining the work closely enough to offer an interpretation the artist can either use as scaffolding or completely tear down.

But structural stewardship requires more than artistic critique. To sustain the work, the creator must survive their own internal engine. The practice forces necessary ego deaths and dismantles narcissistic loops that drain creative stamina. We strip away the parasitic need for external supply and replace it with grounded, sovereign autonomy—killing the narcissism to protect the person, and by extension, the work.

This stewardship spans across mediums, encompassing visual art, sculpture, installation, short stories, plays, scripts, essays, songs, films, and performance—as well as the macro-systems required to keep those practices standing over the long term.

Audience development is part of that infrastructure. We work with artists to build publishing channels and lasting relationships with the people who encounter their work, ensuring the creator dictates the terms of engagement.

Structural honesty

We expose the load-bearing reality of the artist’s situation, examining what the practice can carry and where its limits are being exceeded. We call this conversational brutalism: applying the architectural principle of exposed structure to information. The conversation makes the conditions visible so the artist can decide what to change and where support is needed.

Observation & absence

We come close only to observe and edit, bringing absolute clarity to the raw material. Then we depart. The response, the execution, and the timeline remain entirely sovereign.

We build for asynchronous stamina. Our engagement alternates between high-density structural interventions and extended periods of total absence, giving the artist the isolation required to execute without surveillance.

Institutional stewardship

We work with curators as a defensive buffer between the artist and institutional demands. Together, we examine what an engagement asks of the artist, establish boundaries around access and output, and address pressures that could exhaust the person sustaining the work. Our role as artist developers is to make those boundaries hold.

Ownership & engagement

The artist retains ownership and creative control. Artists can engage us directly, or an institution can commission the work with the artist’s consent.

The Stewardship Subscription reserves availability between client-triggered interventions. Response times, scope, and capacity are agreed in advance. Fixed project commissions and focused advisory engagements are also available.
