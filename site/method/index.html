The manual

The method: diagnosis & scope

Traditional A&R and institutional pipelines can extract creative output faster than an artist's internal and operational infrastructure can carry it. Delivery demands then exceed the capacity required to sustain the work.

Wild-Lake is the structural counterpart. We build the conditions, capacity, and artistic rigor required for work that endures. An engagement may be initiated by the artist or commissioned by a manager, label, gallery, or agency. The artist enters willingly and retains control of the work.

The practice begins with the assets the artist already holds: raw material, rights, skills, audience relationships, and working infrastructure. We identify what those assets can support, agree an intervention, observe it in use, and revise it against the result. Wild-Lake takes 0% IP ownership and 0% revenue share.

Terms of the practice

Structural design

Organizing time, space, resources, habits, and boundaries so the work can be made and sustained.

Talent architecture

Fortifying the person carrying the work: their judgment, capacity, autonomy, and relationships, within those working conditions.

Sovereign A&R

Multidisciplinary Artist & Repertoire: how we scout talent and develop complete bodies of work across music, visual art, literature, and film through critique, direction, and critical framing. The artist retains ownership and creative control.

The mechanics of talent architecture

Wild-Lake builds you a private Yankee Stadium instead of a pop-up tent. We engineer permanent, load-bearing infrastructure designed to hold your output, your schedule, and your creative weight without collapsing when the season gets heavy. Unlike a public arena built for spectators, this structure exists strictly for one artist's practice: private, durable, and entirely yours.

Talent architecture treats a career as an interdependent system of creative assets, working conditions, capacity, and commitments. The four orbital principles below define what we examine and how the artist builds durable career equity.

1. Core mass

Condense the raw material before exposing it to market pressure. We protect studio time, research, rehearsal, and revision so the work develops density and artistic rigor. Market exposure follows material the artist can stand behind and infrastructure that can carry the response.

2. Orbital velocity

Calibrate output to the artist's actual capacity. We examine the release schedule, workload, income requirements, and recovery time, then set working rhythms, access boundaries, and decision responsibilities. Capacity and delivery are reviewed together so output does not consume the person sustaining it.

3. Elliptical cycles

Use both public and quiet phases intentionally. Perihelion describes periods of release, exhibition, production, and live performance. Aphelion describes research, rest, and raw gestation. We scope commitments and resources for each phase so quiet development contributes to the next body of work rather than being treated as failure.

4. Escape velocity

Build enough independent capacity to leave predatory deals, reactive trends, and draining environments. Retained rights, direct audience relationships, and working infrastructure generate leverage without surrendering equity. Wild-Lake takes 0% IP ownership and 0% revenue share, so the artist retains the assets and decisions required to change course.

Execution: demolition, critique, honesty, cadence

Controlled demolition

Audit the practice with the artist. Identify the brittle systems, inherited commitments, and habits that exceed available capacity. Agree what to remove, retain, or rebuild, then establish working conditions that protect the artist's autonomy. Test the changes in actual work before treating them as settled.

Critique & revision

Study raw material closely across visual art, sculpture, installation, writing, music, film, and performance. Offer direct interpretations and revisions that the artist can test, use, or reject. Examine ego loops and dependence on external validation when they drain stamina or distort decisions. Evaluate changes through the work itself.

Conversational brutalism

Expose the load-bearing limits without euphemism: what the practice can carry, what it costs to deliver, and where commitments exceed available time, resources, or physical capacity. Make those conditions explicit so the artist can decide what changes and what support is required.

Observation & absence

Alternate high-density interventions with total absence from day-to-day execution. We observe, critique, and agree the intervention, then step back so the artist works without surveillance. At the next agreed review, artist and studio examine what happened and refine the intervention through use.

Institutional alignment: managers, agents, labels & curators

Wild-Lake works with managers, booking agents, labels, galleries, curators, and A&R executives as a defensive buffer and developmental engine around the artist. We do not replace those roles. We examine how institutional demands affect the capacity, artistic rigor, and long-term value of the underlying creative asset.

Before work begins, the artist, representative, and studio agree the scope, decision responsibilities, delivery timeline, access boundaries, and review points. The artist confirms participation and determines what material or information may be shared. A third-party commission creates no claim on the artist's work.

When demands exceed capacity, we make the conflict explicit and revise the scope, resources, or timeline with the parties involved. Delivery is planned against actual conditions. Each review checks whether those boundaries hold, whether the intervention supports the work, and what must change to prevent over-extraction.

What our involvement means

Our judgment develops through sustained attention, critique, and working together. We can speak to the commitment to craft, care for the person sustaining it, and respect for ownership that we have encountered in that work. Curators, labels, publishers, producers, collectors, and institutions can draw on that account when considering a working relationship with the artist. We cannot guarantee an artist's future.

Meet the artists and creators

Governance & RACI

Wild-Lake provides external critique and structural architecture, never competing authority. Before any institutional engagement begins, we establish a written governance map with the artist, management, and label defining who holds final sign-off across repertoire, visual direction, rollout, and touring.

Artist wellbeing & development protocols

The pace, boundaries, and expectations of an engagement account for the artist's history, physical experience, and present capacity. The artist decides what to share and can pause, decline, or change direction at any time.

We support artists rebuilding self-trust after narcissistic abuse, examining narcissistic patterns within themselves, or both. The work examines harmful relational patterns and establishes boundaries that protect creative autonomy. Trauma-informed pacing leaves room for care beyond the studio.

During a retained engagement, Argelys may guide somatic, creative, or operational practices selected with the artist. Touch and co-regulation are optional and require explicit, ongoing consent. Participation in a practice is never a condition of retaining ownership or creative control.

Outcomes & proof

A healthy creative practice is measured in finished, durable output, not sentiment. Success looks like a coherent body of work, fewer abandoned projects, a release cadence the artist can sustain without burning out, and a live show built to survive a tour.

Development protocols

Active listening, structural honesty, automatic writing, grounding, movement, and vision quests, alongside operational systems for inquiries, communication, payments, and direct publishing. Each practice is optional. The artist can pause, decline, or change direction.

Somatic and diagnostic exercises are optional tools deployed only when the work requires them, never a required curriculum. Protocols are tailored to the artist's physical schedule, conducted in-person in Brooklyn, remotely, in the studio, or on the road, in focused 60 to 90 minute working blocks.

Ownership, Universe API & governance

The artist retains 100% of their IP and creative control. Wild-Lake takes 0% IP ownership and 0% revenue share. Asset-based leverage remains with the artist, including rights, income, and direct audience relationships.

An upfront monthly Stewardship subscription reserves agreed capacity for client-triggered interventions. Scope, response windows, access limits, and review points are set before the engagement begins. Fixed commissions and focused advisory scopes are available when the work has a defined boundary.

Universe API is Argelys's macro-micro diagnostic framework. It connects the conditions of the wider system with the artist's day-to-day capacity and decisions. It governs how interventions are scoped, tested in real conditions, and revised through use.

Each intervention follows a closed loop: identify the constraint and the assets available, agree the change and the evidence to review, deploy it, observe its effect on the practice, and refine it with the artist. The review governs the next intervention and keeps the agreed scope proportional to the work.
