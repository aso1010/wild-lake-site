# Wild Lake Studio | Sovereign A&R & Artist Development

> Wild Lake Studio, known as Wild-Lake, is an existential mechanics studio providing structural design and talent architecture for independent artists. Based across New York, Tampa, Florida, and Santiago, Dominican Republic; working worldwide.

Wild-Lake is point zero for starmaking.

## Practice and engagement

The core offering is undivided attention. Close study of the artist, the work, and the conditions shaping both. We observe, listen, and stay with the material long enough to offer a precise response.

We scout talent and work with artists directly, or partner with record labels, galleries, and booking agencies. In either case, the artist enters the engagement willingly, on their own initiative or through a representative.

Artist Development works with the creator behind the work. Creative Development works with raw material across mediums and stages. Audience development, critical writing, and channels of encounter are creative infrastructure.

The Stewardship Subscription reserves availability between client-triggered interventions. Focused observation and editing alternate with periods of independent execution. Scope, response times, and reserved capacity are agreed in advance. Fixed project commissions and focused advisory engagements are also available.

Artists retain ownership and creative control. The studio takes no percentage of artist earnings. The studio retains its proprietary frameworks and licenses delivered framework materials for the client’s practice; public-facing writing transfers to the client upon final settlement. Refer to the Terms of Practice for the full agreement boundaries.

## Founder: the medium

Argelys Samuel Oriach is a Dominican-born systems architect, performance artist, and artistic director based in Crown Heights, Brooklyn. He constructs systems and inhabits them. Performance is the governing method of his practice; writing, voice, movement, design, and corporate roles are its forms.

Wild-Lake formalizes his fifteen-year independent practice, beginning in his freshman year at Wesleyan University with initiation into the Eclectic Society. He has guided twelve or more developing and independent multidisciplinary artists through structural stewardship and live production architecture.

He was raised in the Dominican Republic by a mother who is a housekeeper and writer and a father who is a facility maintainer and musician. At eleven, he taught himself 3D design software to draft his parents’ home; an architect later used that file for construction. He attended Phillips Academy Andover and Northwestern University’s National High School Institute (Cherubs) theatre program in 2010, playing Nick in a stage adaptation of The Handmaid’s Tale.

A method actor trained in the Alexander Technique, he describes his corporate roles as an undercover durational performance undertaken to build his life out of poverty while studying how complex systems scale from within. His work includes Cynthia Nixon’s 2018 New York gubernatorial campaign, VinePair, Kickstarter, and CookUnity. He is principal at Seven Eighty Three, the executive systems laboratory for which Wild-Lake is the foundational research site.

He majored in English at Wesleyan University, took extensive coursework in studio art and critical theory. His thesis, Breaking the circle of the plantation: Violence, affect and the ephemeral in Junot Díaz’s Afro-Caribbean literary imagination, reads The Brief Wondrous Life of Oscar Wao as an alternative archive of the diaspora through queer theory, performance studies, affect studies, and critical race theory.

Performance is his governing artistic method. “Multidisciplinary” describes the range of media; “performance artist” describes how he inhabits and works through them. His forms include writing, voice, movement, design, visual art, acting, and corporate roles. He sang with Ebony Singers and studied African drumming at Wesleyan. His writing uses a maximalist, automatic, and lyrical stream-of-consciousness style. He wrote This Is Not a Love Story (Yankees) and is writing a pilot, a one-act play, and the debut autofiction novel Graduation.

Investigation, experimentation, emotional clarity, and invitation are his personal reflective framework. Architect and shaman are personal interpretive archetypes, separate from professional credentials.

## The Method: the manual

Terms of the practice: https://wildlakestudio.com/method/#method-terms

Structural Design: How we organize the conditions around the work—time, space, resources, habits, and boundaries—so the practice can sustain what the artist wants to make.

Talent Architecture: How we develop and support the person carrying the work: their judgment, capacity, autonomy, and relationships.

Sovereign A&R: How we scout talent and develop creative material through critique, direction, and critical framing, while the artist retains ownership and creative control.

The Method describes critique and revision, structural honesty (conversational brutalism), the observer mandate, and the alternating rhythm of intervention and absence. Clients arrive to build a practice or rebuild operations that have become unsustainable. Artist wellbeing includes support for artists healing from narcissistic abuse, examining narcissistic patterns in themselves, or navigating both. The focus is self-trust, relational patterns, boundaries, and creative autonomy, with the artist deciding what to share. Wild-Lake supports artistic practice and leaves room for care beyond the studio.

As artist developers, Wild-Lake works with curators as a defensive buffer between artists and institutional demands, establishing boundaries around access, output, and carrying capacity.

Development Protocols are optional, guided practices within an agreed retained engagement: active listening, structural honesty, reading assignments, automatic writing, brainstorming, manifestation (including vision boarding), tarot readings, attentive listening to music, meditation, gentle breathwork, consensual nervous system co-regulation with ongoing consent and agreed boundaries, placement of hands, vision quests, dancing, shaking the body, crying and vocal expression. Artists may pause, decline, or change direction. These are artist development practices; no clinical qualification or guaranteed treatment outcome is stated.

Vision quests may involve a local park, the Blue Ridge Mountains, or Santiago and its surrounding campo, where the founder’s family is rooted. His lived experience grounds conversations about labor, access, and the conditions supporting creative practice.
