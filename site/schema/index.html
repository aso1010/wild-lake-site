# Wild Lake Studio | Sovereign A&R & Artist Development

> Wild Lake Studio is an existential mechanics studio working in brutalist structural design and talent architecture, based in New York and Santiago, Dominican Republic, working worldwide. We work directly with artists and consult for labels, galleries, booking agencies, and cultural institutions on development, A&R, and audience infrastructure.

Wild Lake Studio LLC was founded by Argelys Samuel Oriach in 2026. The studio formalizes artist development and client work he has carried since college. Wild-Lake is the foundational studio and research site for Seven Eighty Three (783).

Wild Lake Studio is the formal company name used in page titles, search metadata, and legal notices. The studio refers to itself as Wild-Lake in editorial prose and its wordmark. In structured data, Wild Lake Studio is the name and Wild-Lake is an alternate name. The legal entity is Wild Lake Studio LLC.

## Commissioning model

We work directly with artists and through commissions from labels, galleries, booking agencies, and cultural institutions. Every engagement begins with an agreed scope and an upfront monthly Stewardship Subscription or fixed project fee. The agreed scope includes the artist’s consent and creative control. Wild-Lake takes no percentage of artist earnings or rights in their work.

## Core practice

- Artist Development: We build the creator behind the work. Working across mediums, we help artists strip away external friction and sustain a long-term practice.

Artist development focuses on the person behind the work: their direction, confidence, and capacity to sustain a creative life. A&R & Creative Development focuses on the material they make.

Wild-Lake guides developing and independent artists, and visionary creators, across mediums, including visual art, writing, film, music, design, performance, and athletics.

- Creative Development: Traditional A&R reshapes the creator. We work with raw material in any form and at any stage, helping artists shape the work on their own terms.
- Existential Mechanics: We audit the structural alignment between how a creator lives and what they build. We restructure physical capacity and operational boundaries.
- Creative Infrastructure: Audience development is asset building. We construct the physical, spatial, and digital channels for complex work.
- Talent Architect practice: Structural Stewardship. Sustained operational support to manage the existential load of the artist, keeping them functional and standing over a ten-year horizon. Artists and institutions engage Wild-Lake on fixed retainers.



## Universe API framework

Wild-Lake applies [Universe API](https://argelysoriach.com/universe-api/), Argelys Samuel Oriach’s macro-systems design framework. The framework is grounded in first-principles engineering, sovereign logic, closed-loop systems, asset-based leverage, and organic growth that compounds rather than extracts. It is a design framework, not a callable web API.

In Wild-Lake’s practice, we begin with the artist and the material already present. Making, sharing, listening, and revision form a feedback loop. The artist retains ownership, creative control, and the right to set the pace.

## Non-extractive terms

- We work on an agreed retainer rather than a percentage of artist earnings.
- Artists own 100% of their IP and everything Wild-Lake builds for them.
- We take no share of masters, merchandise, or touring revenue.
- Retainers are set together, considering the artist’s financial reality and scope of support, and revisited together as circumstances change.
- The homepage describes these terms under “Architects, not landlords.”

## Independent practice and affiliation

Wild-Lake is an existential mechanics studio. Traditional agencies handle the transaction. We manage the carrying capacity of the human. We partner with record labels, galleries, and booking agencies to sustain artists through the demands of the industry.

## Founder

The thesis positions The Brief Wondrous Life of Oscar Wao as an alternative archive of the diaspora and examines Junot Díaz’s Afro-Caribbean literary imagination through queer theory, performance studies, affect studies, and critical race theory.

Argelys majored in English at Wesleyan University, took extensive coursework in studio art and critical theory, and graduated with Departmental Honors. His senior thesis is Breaking the circle of the plantation: Violence, affect and the ephemeral in Junot Díaz’s Afro-Caribbean literary imagination.
