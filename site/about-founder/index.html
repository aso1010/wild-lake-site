Argelys Samuel Oriach founded Wild-Lake to develop artists and their work.

Argelys is a Dominican-born visual, performance, and movement artist, creative director, actor, vocalist, writer, and systems architect based in Brooklyn, New York. He is the principal at Seven Eighty Three (783), for which Wild-Lake serves as the foundational studio and research site. The studio has bases in New York and Santiago, Dominican Republic, and works worldwide.

The Person and the Work

Argelys Samuel Oriach builds infrastructure for artists under the exact same principles he applies to technical architecture: nothing decorative, and everything carries weight.

His structural approach is rooted in physical staging. At Wesleyan’s Eclectic House, he produced live shows, managed production gear, and handled artist hospitality. That direct experience organizing cultural space and navigating the logistical realities of performance forms the foundation of Wild-Lake.

A method actor trained in the Alexander Technique, he spent over a decade treating his life as a long durational performance—fully inhabiting roles across corporate infrastructure, elite institutions, and artistic practice. Having recently closed that chapter to return to his body and voice, he now engineers systems to help other artists sustain their own practices.

His own life was the first trial of this approach. During his durational performance, he learned how to keep himself standing and develop a practice with few resources, against conditions that made both difficult. That lived experience informs the infrastructure he now builds with other artists.

The Method

At Wild-Lake, development requires close study of the material and aggressive revision with the artist. Critique is the primary mechanism of care. It demands examining the work closely enough to offer an interpretation the artist can either use as scaffolding or completely tear down.

This structural stewardship spans across mediums, encompassing visual art, sculpture, installation, short stories, plays, scripts, essays, songs, films, and performance—as well as the macro-systems required to keep those practices standing over the long term.

Audience development is part of that infrastructure. He works with artists to build publishing channels and lasting relationships with the people who encounter their work.

A personal framework.

Argelys understands his approach through the architect and shaman archetypes. The architect gives the practice structure. The shaman attends to meaning, transition, and the artist’s relationship to their work.

These archetypes inform how he shapes the conditions around an artist while staying close to what the work means to them.

Argelys describes his Human Design profile as a 1/3 Emotional Projector. He uses this as a personal framework for reflecting on investigation, experimentation, emotional clarity, and invitation.

Investigation
Understanding the foundations before proposing a structure. Asking what is present, what matters, and what the work needs.
Experimentation
Learning through making and lived experience. Letting what happens in practice inform the next decision.
Emotional clarity
Allowing time to understand a response rather than treating urgency as direction.
Invitation
Entering a creative relationship by mutual recognition and consent. Guidance is offered within a relationship the artist chooses.
