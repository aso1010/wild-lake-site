About our founder

Invitation.

Wild-Lake was founded by Argelys Samuel Oriach to scout talent, develop creative work, and build structures that support an artist’s independence.

Argelys is a Dominican-born performance artist, actor, vocalist, writer, and systems architect based in Brooklyn, New York. He is the principal at Seven Eighty Three (783), for which Wild-Lake serves as the foundational studio and research site. The studio has bases in New York and Santiago, Dominican Republic, and works worldwide.

The person and the work.

Argelys got his start producing live shows and events as a member of Wesleyan’s historic Eclectic House. From booking acts and sourcing production gear to managing artist hospitality, he spent years designing high-voltage physical gatherings from the ground up. That early hands-on work building autonomous cultural spaces forms the backbone of Wild Lake’s approach to talent architecture today.

A method actor trained in the Alexander Technique, he understands the years following his 2008 application to Phillips Academy Andover as a long durational performance: fully inhabiting roles across institutions, corporate infrastructure, and artistic practice. He describes that chapter as recently closed, with a return to his body and voice making space for Wild-Lake and the next phase of his work.

His practice moves between first-principles investigation and lived experimentation: studying the material, testing ideas, listening to what emerges, and revising with the artist. Guidance begins with an invitation and develops through sustained attention to the person and the work.

Brutalist talent architecture often takes the form of critique as a way to love an artist’s work: taking it seriously enough to examine its choices, expose its conditions, and strengthen what it can carry.

That includes developing the artwork itself—a song, a play, a pilot, a novel, or an emerging body of work. Through the Talent Architect practice, scouting and A&R sit alongside mentorship and system design. The structures exist to support the creator and the creative material.

His own creative practice spans performance art, acting for stage and screen, vocal performance, playwriting, and literary fiction. He is the author of This Is Not a Love Story and is writing a play and his debut autofiction novel, Graduation. Explore his creative profile

Sovereign A&R.

For Wild-Lake, sovereignty means that the artist keeps ownership, creative control, and the right to choose their direction. We develop the work and build the infrastructure around it without taking a percentage of the artist’s success.

Argelys’s background in brand marketing, audience development, and systems design informs this approach. We investigate the central questions, test ideas in practice, and use what we learn to shape the next iteration. We work on an agreed retainer, with scope and fees discussed together.

Spirit and engineering.

The Universe API is Argelys’s macro-systems design framework. It brings together first-principles engineering, sovereign logic, closed-loop systems, and growth that compounds rather than extracts.

At Wild-Lake, that becomes a practical cycle: make, share, listen, revise. We begin with the artist’s existing material and resources, then build structures that can hold the work as it develops. Sharing is part of understanding the work, through ourselves and through others.

A personal framework.

Argelys describes his Human Design profile as a 1/3 Emotional Projector. He uses this as a personal framework for reflecting on investigation, experimentation, emotional clarity, and invitation.

Investigation
Understanding the foundations before proposing a structure. Asking what is present, what matters, and what the work needs.
Experimentation
Learning through making and lived experience. Letting what happens in practice inform the next decision.
Emotional clarity
Allowing time to understand a response rather than treating urgency as direction.
Invitation
Entering a creative relationship by mutual recognition and consent. Guidance is offered within a relationship the artist chooses.
