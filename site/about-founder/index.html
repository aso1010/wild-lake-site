Argelys Samuel Oriach is a Dominican-born systems architect, performance artist, and artistic director based in Crown Heights, Brooklyn. He constructs systems and inhabits them. Performance is the governing method of his practice; writing, voice, movement, design, and corporate roles are its forms. He is the principal at Seven Eighty Three (783), an executive systems laboratory where he builds and sells sovereign infrastructure whole, for which Wild Lake Studio serves as the foundational research site. The studio operates across New York, Tampa, Florida, and Santiago, Dominican Republic, and works worldwide.

Wild Lake Studio is the formalization of Argelys Samuel Oriach’s fifteen-year independent practice. That impulse began during his freshman year at Wesleyan University when he was initiated into the Eclectic Society, producing live shows, organizing cultural space, and working alongside artists.

Over fifteen years, he has guided twelve or more developing and independent multidisciplinary artists, providing the structural stewardship, physical carrying capacity, and live production architecture that formalized into Wild Lake Studio.

The Person and the Work

Argelys Samuel Oriach builds infrastructure for artists under the exact same principles he applies to technical architecture: nothing decorative, and everything carries weight.

Born in the Dominican Republic, he was raised by a mother who is a housekeeper and writer and a father who is a facility maintainer and musician. His structural impulse emerged at age eleven when he taught himself 3D design software to draft his parents' dream home. An architect later used that exact file for actual construction. And in the wake of the 2008 financial crash, he applied to Phillips Academy Andover to consciously design the conditions of his own life.

A method actor trained in the Alexander Technique, he spent the next sixteen years treating his body, voice, and daily life as a single structural instrument. In 2010, he attended Northwestern University’s National High School Institute (Cherubs) theatre program, playing Nick in a stage adaptation of The Handmaid’s Tale. From there, he studied the mechanics of somatic weight, physical posture, and human attention by fully inhabiting roles across elite institutions, physical staging, and corporate infrastructure.

That foundation fueled a twelve-year trajectory building organic growth systems. During that period, he served as Cynthia Nixon’s social media manager for her 2018 campaign for governor of New York, later pivoting to architect sovereign digital frameworks from first principles. He went on to build brand infrastructure at VinePair, direct brand marketing at Kickstarter, and lead organic growth at CookUnity. But he approached his corporate roles as an undercover durational performance. It was a calculated effort to build his life out of poverty while auditing how complex systems scale from the inside out. That performance cycle recently closed with an intentional return to his body, breath, and voice.

He now operates two primary laboratories. Seven Eighty Three is an independent systems design practice engineering automated infrastructure for executive leadership, where he builds and sells sovereign infrastructure whole. And over fifteen years, he guided twelve or more developing and independent multidisciplinary artists, providing the structural stewardship, physical carrying capacity, and live production architecture that formalized into Wild Lake Studio, known as Wild-Lake.

The Creative Practice

Performance governs how he works across writing, voice, movement, visual art, design, and acting for stage and screen. “Multidisciplinary” describes the range of media; “performance artist” describes how he inhabits and works through them. This is his artistic position: he constructs systems and inhabits them.

At Wesleyan, he sang with Ebony Singers, studying African-American religious music through performance, including traditional and contemporary gospel, spirituals, and hymns. He also took African drumming courses.

As a writer, he applies this same somatic and structural rigor to narrative through a maximalist, automatic, and lyrical stream-of-consciousness style. His focus remains on the underlying physical and digital mechanics across every medium.

Education

He majored in English at Wesleyan University, where he took extensive coursework in studio art and critical theory. His senior thesis was Breaking the circle of the plantation: Violence, affect and the ephemeral in Junot Díaz’s Afro-Caribbean literary imagination.

The thesis positions The Brief Wondrous Life of Oscar Wao as an alternative archive of the diaspora and examines Junot Díaz’s Afro-Caribbean literary imagination through queer theory, performance studies, affect studies, and critical race theory.

A personal framework.

Argelys understands his approach through the architect and shaman archetypes. The architect gives the practice structure. The shaman attends to meaning, transition, and the artist’s relationship to their work. Together, these approaches make him an alchemist, transforming raw material and lived experience into artistic form.

These archetypes inform how he shapes the conditions around an artist while staying close to what the work means to them.

Argelys uses a personal framework to reflect on investigation, experimentation, emotional clarity, and invitation.

Investigation
Understanding the foundations before proposing a structure. Asking what is present, what matters, and what the work needs.
Experimentation
Learning through making and lived experience. Letting what happens in practice inform the next decision.
Emotional clarity
Allowing time to understand a response rather than treating urgency as direction.
Invitation
Entering a creative relationship by mutual recognition and consent. Guidance is offered within a relationship the artist chooses.
