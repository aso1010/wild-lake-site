Film, creative direction & performance

Aissa Rose
Gueye.

Senegalese-American writer-director, creative director, actress, and model raised in New York City and based in Los Angeles. Her practice spans film, photography, writing, and performance.

The writer-director

Writing and image.

Aissa Rose Gueye is a writer-director, creative director, actress, and model working across film, photography, and performance. She has worked as a writer’s assistant and script coordinator on RAP SH!T (seasons 1 and 2), Random Acts of Flyness (season 2), and We Stay Looking (season 1).

Her film and photo work has been featured by Issa Rae Presents, Afropunk, The Photographic Journal, and Toksick Magazine, and at BlackStar Film Festival, Black Harvest Film Festival, and Montreal International Black Film Festival.

After years helping bring other people’s stories into being, Gueye is finding her own voice as a writer-director. Her practice carries the experience of that collaborative work into the question of what she wants to say in her own name.

Raised in New York City, she earned her BA in Film and African-American Studies at Wesleyan University. She lives in Los Angeles.

Performance, writing & music

Argelys Samuel
Oriach.

Argelys Samuel Oriach is a Dominican-born visual, performance, and movement artist, creative director, actor, writer, vocalist, and songwriter. He is the author of This Is Not a Love Story and is writing a pilot, a play, and his debut autofiction novel, Graduation.

The person & the work ↓
The artist

Character.
Voice. Story.

Raised in poverty by a homemaker and writer and a mechanical engineer working in maintenance who is also a vocalist, Oriach understands creative capacity through the conditions that allow someone to pursue it. A method actor trained in the Alexander Technique, he studies how people look, listen, move, and affect one another.

He understands his years inhabiting institutional, corporate, and artistic roles as a durational performance undertaken to design his life and his family’s life out of poverty. Those material stakes give his work its questions of survival, self-fashioning, trust, concealment, and authorship. That chapter closed with a return to his body and voice.

He attended Phillips Academy Andover and majored in English at Wesleyan University, studying studio art and critical theory and graduating with Departmental Honors. Wild-Lake formalizes the artist development work he has carried since college.

Drew
Rutland.

New York–based producer, fashion designer, and stylist working with high-profile talent and fashion brands. His background spans VIP and influencer relations, event production, and fashion design.

The person & the work ↓
The producer, designer & stylist

Image and encounter.

Drew Rutland is a fashion designer, stylist, and producer connecting fashion brands with celebrity and influencer talent. His work includes press days, runway shows, presentations, and the relationships through which a collection enters public view.

His experience includes VIP and influencer roles at Lindsey Media, MVPR, and MODEWORLD, alongside freelance work with Novella Paghera PR. Across those roles, he has worked with brands including Sandy Liang, Wiederhoeft, Dauphinette, Nanushka, and Akoni Group.

His production background includes New York and Paris Fashion Week events, talent arrangements, and the negotiation of campaign budgets, usage rights, and deliverables. That work sits alongside his own artistic practice and education in fashion design.

He earned his BFA in Fashion/Apparel Design at Savannah College of Art and Design, where he studied from 2013 to 2017.

Food writing & editorial

Emily
Wilson.

Food writer and author of The Angel, an IACP Award-winning newsletter about food, restaurants, and the people who shape how we eat.

Visit Emily’s website
The writer

At the table.
In the story.

Emily Wilson writes about food and the people behind it. Her work spans features, profiles, guides, and odes.

She is the author of The Angel, an IACP Award-winning newsletter exploring food, restaurants, and the people who shape how we eat.

Wilson directs attention with editorial discernment. Her choices shape how work is encountered and valued in food culture, bringing taste and access into view and opening questions about capitalism and whiteness.

The title holds the benevolent messenger alongside the language of angel investment. Wilson is the daughter of an angel investor, a connection that gives her editorial role a further resonance: choosing where attention goes can create value for others. We see an awareness of that power in the practice. Its generosity opens the question of whether influence is transferred to the people featured or lent through the writer’s endorsement, and what remains with them as that attention moves on.

Wilson attended Elisabeth Irwin High School and double-majored in English and Sociology at Wesleyan University.

Animation, sculpture & installation

Jessica
Wilson.

Multidisciplinary artist exploring how virtual systems register in bodies, objects, and everyday infrastructure. Born in New York City, she lives in Amsterdam and NYC.

Jessica at diez gallery
The artist

Unseen forces.
Physical sensation.

Jessica Wilson works across computer-generated animation, sculpture, and installation. Her background in history informs her attention to infrastructure within and beyond American borders, and how external systems shape inner experience.

Her practice gives everyday infrastructure an imagined inner life. Anthropomorphized outlets and repurposed New York City taxi tops become surrogates for anxiety, neurosis, and human inexactitude. From a distance, an outlet appears ordinary; closer inspection reveals a private emotional life made public.

Wilson’s anxious objects evoke the psychological effects of capitalism and whiteness, including the tension between benefiting from a system and registering its effects. Their awkwardness gives form to the ways synthetic infrastructure imitates and displaces the living world.

In Fountain (Weeping Outlet), water makes a familiar fixture appear to weep. An imagined inner state enters shared space, shaping what others notice and feel.

She majored in History at Wesleyan University and is an alum of the New York Arts Practicum.

Jimmy Andrew
O’Keeffe.

New York–based producer and filmmaker. He is a producer at North Six. His work includes projects for Nike and Frédéric Malle.

Visit Jimmy’s website
The producer & filmmaker

Producing the work.

Jimmy Andrew O’Keeffe is a producer and filmmaker working across photo and video production. His practice includes executive production, casting, location scouting, art buying, and special projects. He is a producer at North Six, with clients including Nike and Frédéric Malle.

His practice straddles commercial production and artistic filmmaking with a consistent creative vision, carried through commissioned work for brands and the intimate narratives of independent film.

He produced The Sun Is In My Eyes (2024), directed and co-produced by his partner, Pablo Tapia Pla. The short follows a young queer man remembering a past love.

He attended Milton Academy and Tufts University before transferring to Wesleyan, where he majored in Film. His senior thesis was a short film based on Franny and Zooey, with Argelys Samuel Oriach serving as art assistant.

Roach.

The person & the work ↓
The actor & model

Character and presence.

Kyle Roach is an actor for stage and screen and a model whose path began in music production at Capitol Records. Across those roles, he has worked behind the scenes and in front of the camera. Acting now gives him a way to bring that experience into character, with the body and voice as his material.

Roach brings a singular charm and charisma to an industry that often rewards familiarity. His tenacity shows in his willingness to change direction, take risks, and meet challenges without retreating. That appetite for change gives his presence an edge: he is willing to leave familiar ground when he sees the possibility of something greater.

Educated at The Dalton School in New York City, he majored in African-American Studies at Oberlin College.

Fibers & ceramics

Patrick
Schlotterback.

Brooklyn-based artist working in fibers and ceramics. A Studio Technician at Brooklyn Clay since 2016, his practice brings material work together with the technical infrastructure of the studio.

The person & the work ↓
The artist & technician

Material and making.

Patrick Schlotterback works across fibers and ceramics. Since 2016, he has worked as a Studio Technician at Brooklyn Clay.

His earlier experience includes kiln operation and firing at Greenwich House Pottery, research assistance in MICA’s Ceramics Department, and studio work with Kenya (Robinson), Nicholas Guagnini, and Robert Raphael. His technical background spans ceramic and textile fabrication, woodworking, metal fabrication, mold making, and digital fabrication.

Feeling and emotion take form through materials that carry his aesthetic imprint openly. His brutalism lies in that visible authorship: the material becomes unmistakably his, giving an inner life a physical presence through the choices of the maker.

His exhibition history includes Slow Mouth and If Then, With Plenty at Maryland Institute College of Art, Still Alive at Bodega Gallery in Baltimore in 2015, and Pottery Barns at Annex 2E in Baltimore in 2014.

He earned his BFA from Maryland Institute College of Art in Baltimore in 2015, with a double major in Fibers and Ceramics. He also participated in the New York Arts Practicum in 2015.

Design & music

Zachary Reed
Kantor.

New York City–based design director and musician. Working across product, branding, graphic, and motion design, he leads design at GIPHY.

Visit Zachary’s website
The designer & musician

Visual clarity.
Cultural resonance.

Zachary Reed Kantor specializes in UI/UX, branding, and the interplay between them. His practice brings together product design, hand-drawn typography, illustration, and motion.

At GIPHY, he has designed the app, website, and experiences across the platform. He previously helped create Tapslash, an iPhone keyboard acquired by GIPHY in 2018 that became its SDK.

A fixture of New York’s indie rock scene, Kantor plays in Loose Buttons and Rebounder. He also works with musicians and artists on album artwork, show posters, and vinyl packaging. He created Treble.fm to help music makers find collaborators.

Kantor’s practice explores artistic self-fashioning through the body. Stance and gesture convey mastery, carrying the traces of rehearsal and technique into an image of the artist at work. That bohemian image evokes the material conditions of creative freedom, including the money, privilege, and security that make room for a sustained practice.

Kantor completed high school at Columbia Grammar and Prep School and pursued a bachelor’s degree in Econometrics and Music at Wesleyan University.
