Writing, film & photography

Aissa Rose
Gueye.

Senegalese-American writer-director raised in New York City and based in Los Angeles. Her practice spans film, photography, and writing.

Visit Aissa’s website
The writer-director

Writing and image.

Aissa Rose Gueye works across film and photography. She has worked as a writer’s assistant and script coordinator on RAP SH!T (seasons 1 and 2), Random Acts of Flyness (season 2), and We Stay Looking (season 1).

Her film and photo work has been featured by Issa Rae Presents, Afropunk, The Photographic Journal, and Toksick Magazine, and at BlackStar Film Festival, Black Harvest Film Festival, and Montreal International Black Film Festival.

After years helping bring other people’s stories into being, Gueye is finding her own voice as a writer-director. Her practice carries the experience of that collaborative work into the question of what she wants to say in her own name.

Raised in New York City, she earned her BA in Film and African-American Studies at Wesleyan University. She lives in Los Angeles.

Performance, writing & music

Argelys Samuel
Oriach.

Dominican-born visual and performance artist, actor for stage and screen, writer, vocalist, and songwriter working across theatre, literary fiction, and music. Oriach is currently writing a play and his debut novel.

The person & the work ↓
The artist

Character.
Voice. Story.

Argelys Samuel Oriach is a Dominican-born visual and performance artist, actor, writer, vocalist, and songwriter. A method actor trained in the Alexander Technique, he works with the mechanics of attention: how people look, listen, move, and affect one another.

He understands his years inhabiting institutional, corporate, and artistic roles as a durational performance, shaped by the effort to design his life and his family’s life out of poverty. That chapter recently closed with a return to his body and voice.

His practice explores the relationship between survival and self-fashioning. Designing a way out of poverty gives the durational performance its material stakes, while the roles themselves bring questions of trust, concealment, and authorship into view.

He is the author of This Is Not a Love Story and is writing a play and his debut autofiction novel, Graduation.

Visit Emily’s website
The writer

At the table.
In the story.

Emily Wilson writes about food and the people behind it. Her work spans features, profiles, guides, and odes.

She is the author of The Angel, an IACP Award-winning newsletter exploring food, restaurants, and the people who shape how we eat.

The Angel brings the power of editorial attention into view. A profile can change how a person’s work is encountered and valued. Its choices of figures and brands evoke questions about taste, access, capitalism, and whiteness within food culture.

The title’s benevolent messenger gives that attention a particular charge. Admiration can gather around both the subject and the person making them visible. For us, the work opens a question about whether power is transferred or lent through that encounter, and what remains with the subject after attention moves elsewhere.

Animation, sculpture & installation

Jessica
Wilson.

Multidisciplinary artist exploring how virtual systems register in bodies, objects, and everyday infrastructure. Born in New York City, she lives in Amsterdam and NYC.

Jessica at diez gallery
The artist

Unseen forces.
Physical sensation.

Jessica Wilson works across computer-generated animation, sculpture, and installation. Through light, color, and spatial relationships, she makes the bodily and material effects of virtual systems tangible.

Her study of history at Wesleyan informs her attention to infrastructure within and beyond American borders. Her work explores how these external systems shape inner experience, giving historical forces a bodily and psychological presence.

Her practice gives everyday infrastructure an imagined inner life. Anthropomorphized electrical outlets and repurposed New York City taxi tops become surrogates for anxiety, neurosis, and human inexactitude.

Wilson’s anxious objects evoke the psychological effects of capitalism and whiteness, including the tension between living within a system and registering its effects. Their awkwardness gives physical form to the ways synthetic infrastructure imitates and displaces the living world.

Her work also concerns the reach of an inner life into shared space. An imagined psychological state becomes a physical encounter that shapes what others notice and feel. Siren brings the resources behind that transformation into view, opening questions about who has the wealth or institutional backing to build infrastructure and give private experience a public presence.

Athletics · Baseball

Jordy
Oriach.

Left-handed power. An unconventional path from the Dominican Republic through junior college, New Mexico, and the University of Georgia. Built through persistence and progression.

The path & the player ↓
The athlete

Built
differently.

Jordy Oriach attended high school in the Dominican Republic before playing at Frank Phillips College and New Mexico. In 2025, he led the Mountain West in hits, total bases, doubles, runs, on-base percentage, and slugging percentage.

He went on to play for Georgia in 2026.

Oriach’s practice gives humility, persistence, and hope a physical form through daily attention to craft. His path evokes the relationship between an athlete’s commitment to the game and the recognition institutions such as MLB confer. The meaning of that commitment extends through the work of playing itself.

Sophia
Jennings.

Los Angeles–based writer and filmmaker. Director of Story at MakeMake, working across film, editorial, and advertising.

Visit Sophia’s website
The writer

Story.
Across forms.

Sophia Jennings is a Los Angeles–based writer and filmmaker. She works across film, interviews, and advertising, with story at the center.

She is Director of Story at MakeMake. Her films have screened internationally, and her interviews appear in Harper’s BAZAAR, Rolling Stone, and ESQUIRE.

Born in San Francisco, she studied in London and at Wesleyan.

Jennings’s writing gives self-love a form through the attention she brings to experience. Drafting and revision make that experience available to others. Her practice connects trust in her own voice with curiosity about lives beyond it.

Design & music

Zachary Reed
Kantor.

New York City–based design director and musician. Working across product, branding, graphic, and motion design, he leads design at GIPHY.

Visit Zachary’s website
The designer & musician

Visual clarity.
Cultural resonance.

Zachary Reed Kantor specializes in UI/UX, branding, and the interplay between them. His practice brings together product design, hand-drawn typography, illustration, and motion.

At GIPHY, he has designed the app, website, and experiences across the platform. He previously helped create Tapslash, an iPhone keyboard acquired by GIPHY in 2018 that became its SDK.

A musician who has toured in multiple bands, he also works with musicians and artists on album artwork, show posters, and vinyl packaging. He created Treble.fm to help music makers find collaborators.

Kantor’s practice explores artistic self-fashioning through the body. Stance and gesture convey mastery, carrying the traces of rehearsal and technique into an image of the artist at work. That bohemian image evokes the material conditions of creative freedom, including the money, privilege, and security that make room for a sustained practice.
