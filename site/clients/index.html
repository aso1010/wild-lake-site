Argelys Samuel
Oriach.

Dominican-born performance artist, actor for stage and screen, writer, vocalist, and songwriter working across theatre, literary fiction, and music. Oriach is currently writing a play and his debut novel.

The person & the work ↓
The artist

Character.
Voice. Story.

Argelys Samuel Oriach is a Dominican-born performance artist, actor, writer, vocalist, and songwriter. A method actor trained in the Alexander Technique, he works with the mechanics of attention: how people look, listen, move, and affect one another.

He understands his years inhabiting institutional, corporate, and artistic roles as a durational performance, shaped by the effort to design his life and his family’s life out of poverty. That chapter recently closed with a return to his body and voice.

The practice holds survival and self-fashioning alongside questions of consent, trust, and responsibility: where inhabiting a role becomes concealment, and how others’ accounts complicate the artist’s own.

He is the author of This Is Not a Love Story and is writing a play and his debut autofiction novel, Graduation.

The writer

At the table.
In the story.

Emily Wilson writes about food and the people behind it. Her work spans features, profiles, guides, and odes.

She is the author of The Angel, an IACP Award-winning newsletter exploring food, restaurants, and the people who shape how we eat.

We read The Angel as an editorial practice that gives attention cultural and commercial value. Its choices place particular figures and brands in view while leaving others outside the frame. Taste, access, capitalism, and whiteness inform that field of visibility.

The title’s benevolent messenger introduces a tension between admiration for the writer and attention to the subject. We use white-savior logic to describe the risk of the person offering visibility becoming the protagonist of someone else’s story. Accountability rests partly in recognizing the subject’s agency and remaining open to their response.

Animation, sculpture & installation

Jessica
Wilson.

Multidisciplinary artist exploring how virtual systems register in bodies, objects, and everyday infrastructure. Born in New York City, she lives in Amsterdam and NYC.

Jessica at diez gallery
The artist

Unseen forces.
Physical sensation.

Jessica Wilson works across computer-generated animation, sculpture, and installation. Through light, color, and spatial relationships, she makes the bodily and material effects of virtual systems tangible.

Her practice gives everyday infrastructure an imagined inner life. Anthropomorphized electrical outlets and repurposed New York City taxi tops become surrogates for anxiety, neurosis, and human inexactitude.

We read Wilson’s anxious objects through the psychological effects of capitalism and whiteness, including the tension between benefiting from a system and registering its damage. Their awkwardness gives physical form to the ways synthetic infrastructure imitates and displaces the living world.

Her work also directs attention: an inner state becomes a physical encounter that affects what others notice and feel. Money and institutional backing help determine whose interior life acquires that reach. Siren makes the resources required to build infrastructure part of our encounter with it.

Athletics · Baseball

Jordy
Oriach.

Left-handed power. An unconventional path from the Dominican Republic through junior college, New Mexico, and the University of Georgia. Built through persistence and progression.

The path & the player ↓
The athlete

Built
differently.

Jordy Oriach attended high school in the Dominican Republic before playing at Frank Phillips College and New Mexico. In 2025, he led the Mountain West in hits, total bases, doubles, runs, on-base percentage, and slugging percentage.

He went on to play for Georgia in 2026.

We read Oriach’s practice through humility, persistence, and hope. His record reflects daily attention to craft. MLB recognition matters to a career, but the work also has value in the absence of that validation.

Sophia
Jennings.

Los Angeles–based writer and filmmaker. Director of Story at MakeMake, working across film, editorial, and advertising.

Visit Sophia’s website
The writer

Story.
Across forms.

Sophia Jennings is a Los Angeles–based writer and filmmaker. She works across film, interviews, and advertising, with story at the center.

She is Director of Story at MakeMake. Her films have screened internationally, and her interviews appear in Harper’s BAZAAR, Rolling Stone, and ESQUIRE.

Born in San Francisco, she studied in London and at Wesleyan.

We understand Jennings’s writing as a form of self-love. Drafting and revision give her experience a considered form that others can encounter. The practice connects trust in her own voice with curiosity about experiences beyond it.

Design & music

Zachary Reed
Kantor.

New York City–based design director and musician. Working across product, branding, graphic, and motion design, he leads design at GIPHY.

Visit Zachary’s website
The designer & musician

Visual clarity.
Cultural resonance.

Zachary Reed Kantor specializes in UI/UX, branding, and the interplay between them. His practice brings together product design, hand-drawn typography, illustration, and motion.

At GIPHY, he has designed the app, website, and experiences across the platform. He previously helped create Tapslash, an iPhone keyboard acquired by GIPHY in 2018 that became its SDK.

A musician who has toured in multiple bands, he also works with musicians and artists on album artwork, show posters, and vinyl packaging. He created Treble.fm to help music makers find collaborators.

We read Kantor’s practice through artistic posturing and self-fashioning. Stance and gesture convey mastery, with the body carrying evidence of rehearsal and technique. His bohemian image of creative freedom also brings its supporting conditions into view: money, privilege, and the material security needed to sustain the practice.
