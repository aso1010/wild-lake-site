Artist wellbeing

Development Protocols

Artist development includes examining the conditions in which someone can make work. During a retained engagement, Argelys Samuel Oriach may guide practices that bring attention to the artist’s habits, physical experience, and creative direction.

The selection follows the person and the material. Some sessions involve close reading or conversation. Others make room for movement, music, stillness, or expression.

Reflection & Study

Active listening

We give the artist space to say what they have been holding, at their own pace. Our attention follows what they choose to share, allowing room for expression before interpretation or revision begins.

Structural honesty

We expose the load-bearing reality of the artist’s situation, examining what the practice can carry and where its limits are being exceeded. We call this conversational brutalism: applying the architectural principle of exposed structure to information. The conversation makes the conditions visible so the artist can decide what to change and where support is needed.

Reading assignments

Selected texts give us language for the questions emerging in the practice.

Automatic writing

Writing continuously lets associations, images, and questions emerge before editing begins. We return to the material afterward to see what it holds.

Tarot readings

Images and archetypes provide prompts for interpretation, helping the artist articulate associations, tensions, and possible choices.

Listening to music

Attentive listening becomes a study of rhythm, feeling, memory, and how a work holds attention.

Attention & Grounding

Meditation

Time to notice thoughts, sensations, and the conditions surrounding the work.

Breathwork

Gentle attention to breathing and its relationship to movement, voice, and concentration.

Placement of hands

Self-directed contact can provide a point of physical attention. Any guided touch is discussed and agreed beforehand.

Movement & Expression

Vision quests

Stepping outside familiar surroundings gives the artist room to encounter the work from another perspective. Whether walking through a local park or traveling to the Blue Ridge Mountains, a change of setting brings different scales, distances, and rhythms into attention. We use that space to question established patterns and notice what the usual routine leaves out. The setting and pace are agreed with the artist.

Dancing

Movement offers a way to explore rhythm, impulse, pleasure, and physical expression.

Shaking the body

Voluntary movement provides another way to notice and respond to held tension.

Crying and vocal expression

Emotion can have room without becoming an assignment. Tears, sound, or silence may emerge; none is required as evidence of progress.

Within an Engagement

These practices support the artist’s work within an agreed scope. Participation is voluntary, and the artist can pause, decline, or change direction. Each practice returns to a concrete question: what helps this person sustain the work they want to make?
