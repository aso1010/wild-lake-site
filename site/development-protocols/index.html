Artist wellbeing

Development Protocols

Every protocol serves one structural objective: increasing the artist's capacity to sustain demanding creative work without dependency or burnout. Argelys Samuel Oriach, Founder and Artistic Director of Wild-Lake, guides the work within an agreed engagement.

Internal regulation and external armor are two halves of one system. Cognitive rigor, nervous system capacity, and spatial movement support the person doing the work. Inquiry, publishing, payment, and communication systems protect that capacity from external demands. Each intervention addresses an identified bottleneck, is tested in practice, and is revised against what happens.

Scope, Consent & Boundaries

Participation is voluntary. The artist controls pace, proximity, boundaries, and what they share. They can pause, decline, or change direction at any time. Consent is explicit, specific to the practice, and ongoing. Agreement to one practice does not authorize another.

Wild-Lake operates as an artistic and structural practice rather than clinical therapy. These protocols support the conditions of making; clinical diagnosis and treatment sit outside the engagement. Trauma-informed pacing allows for the artist's history and present capacity, including rebuilding self-trust after narcissistic abuse or examining narcissistic patterns within themselves. Care beyond the studio remains available on the artist's terms.

Before a protocol begins, artist and studio agree the constraint, the purpose of the intervention, the boundaries, and what they will observe. After use, they review its effect on the work and on capacity, then retain, revise, or stop it. The next intervention follows that evidence rather than a fixed program.

Cognitive & Editorial Protocols

These protocols develop artistic rigor and editorial precision. Each returns attention to specific material, decisions, and revisions the artist can evaluate.

Active listening

Listen before interpreting. The artist determines what to share and at what pace. We identify the question, distinguish observation from assumption, and check our understanding with the artist before proposing a change.

Conversational brutalism

Expose the load-bearing conditions directly: time, money, commitments, physical capacity, and the limits of the work. The conversation identifies what the practice can carry and what must change. The artist decides how to act on that information.

Close reading

Study selected texts and the artist's material for structure, language, form, and unresolved questions. Make editorial judgments specific enough to test through revision. Return to the revised work to assess what changed.

Automatic writing

Write continuously before editing to surface associations, images, and questions. Revisit the material afterward, identify what warrants development, and test it through deliberate composition or revision.

Structured ideation

Separate idea generation from evaluation. Generate possible directions, then compare them against the artistic question, available resources, and the artist's capacity. Select an experiment and review the resulting material.

Direction & visual mapping

Articulate the direction of the work and the conditions required to pursue it. Use vision boarding to gather images, words, and references into a shared visual map. Convert that map into priorities and next steps, then revise it as the work develops.

Archetypal interpretation

Use images, archetypes, and tarot as prompts for the artist's own interpretation. Distinguish associations from facts, identify tensions or choices, and return any proposed direction to the work for evaluation.

Deep listening

Study rhythm, voice, silence, feeling, and how a work holds attention. Describe what is heard and test those observations against the artist's intention and subsequent revisions.

Somatic & Nervous System Protocols

These practices examine physical attention and nervous system capacity within the working relationship. The artist's reported experience governs the pace and whether a practice continues.

Consensual co-regulation

Use steady presence, active listening, and shared attention only within boundaries agreed with the artist. Discuss pace and proximity before beginning. Touch is optional and requires separate, explicit, ongoing consent. The artist can withdraw consent, pause, or end the practice at any time.

Stillness & observation

Make time to notice thoughts, sensations, and the working environment without requiring a particular state. The artist determines the duration and whether to continue. Review what the observation reveals about the conditions of making.

Breath mechanics

Bring gentle attention to breathing in relation to movement, voice, and concentration. The artist chooses the pace and stops or adjusts when needed. Observe whether the practice supports the specific task rather than treating a breathing pattern as a performance target.

Self-directed & consented contact

Self-directed contact can provide a point of physical attention. Any guided contact is discussed beforehand, including its purpose, location, and limits, and begins only with explicit consent. Consent remains ongoing; the artist can decline, change, or stop contact immediately. Participation never requires touch.

Spatial & Physical Protocols

Spatial and physical practices examine how environment, movement, and voice affect the work. Participation and intensity follow the artist's capacity rather than a predetermined outcome.

Field immersion

Change the environment to examine assumptions and working conditions. Field immersion can include vision quests, environmental displacement, and visits to a local park, the Blue Ridge Mountains, or Santiago and the surrounding campo, where Argelys's family is rooted. His experience of rural life and poverty grounds discussion of labor, access, and the material conditions that sustain creative work. Agree the setting, purpose, practical demands, and boundaries with the artist before a visit, then review what the change of environment reveals.

Voluntary movement & rhythm

Use movement or dancing to study rhythm, impulse, spatial relationships, and physical expression. The artist sets the intensity and duration. Relate observations back to the work or the conditions required to make it.

Tension discharge

Use voluntary shaking or other self-directed movement to notice and respond to held tension. The artist chooses whether to participate and how to move. Stop or adjust when requested and review whether the practice supports the task at hand.

Vocal & emotional release

Allow sound, tears, silence, or other expression without prescribing a response. Emotional release is optional and is never evidence required to demonstrate progress. The artist controls what is expressed and whether it becomes material for the work.

Technical & Boundary Infrastructure

These systems are the external armor protecting the internal capacity developed through cognitive, somatic, and physical work. Scope them to the actual demand on the artist's attention, income, access, or direct audience relationships.

Programmatic request gating

Close social media DMs to professional inquiries. Route requests to a structured Tally or Typeform form hosted on the artist's custom domain. Require project scope, a budget that meets the artist's minimum, and acknowledgment of terms before the inquiry reaches the inbox. Direct cold contacts to the form through saved replies or automated email responses. Review whether the gate admits relevant requests and protects the artist's attention.

Single-file edge deployments

Deploy portable static HTML and CSS directly to Cloudflare Pages on the artist's own domain. A single-file site can operate without a database, framework dependencies, or a build pipeline. Keep a portable source copy, test the published page, and revise it when the artist's requirements change.

Async communication boundaries

Close open calendar booking. Require a written agenda and decision brief at least 48 hours before a call is booked, including the specific question, relevant material, and decision needed. Book only when the purpose and scope are clear. Review whether calls resolve decisions and preserve time for independent execution.

Direct payment routing

Connect Stripe Payment Links to the artist's own Stripe account, with payouts tied directly to their bank account. Send links to buyers or embed them on the artist's site to bypass marketplace commissions. Payment processing fees still apply. Verify the payment and payout path, and keep the offer and customer relationship under the artist's control.

Custom domain syndicate feeds

Connect a Buttondown newsletter to the artist's custom domain through the required DNS records and publish a native RSS feed on the static site. Maintain exportable subscriber lists and publishing archives for direct audience relationships. Test subscription, delivery, feed access, and export so distribution remains usable beyond any platform.

Universe API governs the shared loop: identify the artist's actual bottleneck, scope the intervention, deploy it in live conditions, observe the result, and revise it through use. Internal practices and technical systems are evaluated together so an external demand does not consume the capacity the artist has built.
