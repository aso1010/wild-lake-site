Artist wellbeing

Development Protocols

Artist development includes examining the conditions in which someone can make work. During a retained engagement, Argelys Samuel Oriach may guide practices that bring attention to the artist’s habits, physical experience, and creative direction.

These practices can support artists rebuilding a sense of safety, self-trust, and boundaries after narcissistic abuse, or examining narcissistic patterns in themselves. The artist sets the pace and chooses what to share.

The selection follows the person and the material. Some sessions involve close reading or conversation. Others make room for movement, music, stillness, or expression.

Reflection & Study

Active listening

We give the artist space to say what they have been holding, at their own pace. Our attention follows what they choose to share, allowing room for expression before interpretation or revision begins.

Structural honesty

We expose the load-bearing reality of the artist’s situation, examining what the practice can carry and where its limits are being exceeded. We call this conversational brutalism: applying the architectural principle of exposed structure to information. The conversation makes the conditions visible so the artist can decide what to change and where support is needed.

Reading assignments

Selected texts give us language for the questions emerging in the practice.

Automatic writing

Writing continuously lets associations, images, and questions emerge before editing begins. We return to the material afterward to see what it holds.

Brainstorming

Open conversation generates ideas, associations, and possible directions before we evaluate them. We return to what emerges to identify what the artist wants to explore.

Manifestation

Articulating the future the artist wants to build, connecting intention with the choices, resources, and daily conditions that can support it.

Vision boarding

Gathering images, words, and references into a visual map of creative direction. The board becomes a shared point of reference for priorities and next steps.

Tarot readings

Images and archetypes provide prompts for interpretation, helping the artist articulate associations, tensions, and possible choices.

Listening to music

Attentive listening becomes a study of rhythm, feeling, memory, and how a work holds attention.

Attention & Grounding

Consensual co-regulation

Steady presence, active listening, and shared attention can support nervous system settling within the working relationship. We agree together on pace, proximity, and boundaries; touch is optional and requires explicit consent. Consent remains ongoing, and the artist can pause, decline, or change direction at any time.

Meditation

Time to notice thoughts, sensations, and the conditions surrounding the work.

Breathwork

Gentle attention to breathing and its relationship to movement, voice, and concentration.

Placement of hands

Self-directed contact can provide a point of physical attention. Any guided touch is discussed and agreed beforehand.

Movement & Expression

Vision quests

A change of surroundings can challenge familiar assumptions. Visits may range from a local park or the Blue Ridge Mountains to Santiago and the surrounding campo, where Argelys’s family is rooted. His experience of rural life and poverty grounds conversations about labor, access, and the conditions that sustain a creative practice. The setting and purpose are agreed with the artist.

Dancing

Movement offers a way to explore rhythm, impulse, pleasure, and physical expression.

Shaking the body

Voluntary movement provides another way to notice and respond to held tension.

Crying and vocal expression

Emotion can have room without becoming an assignment. Tears, sound, or silence may emerge; none is required as evidence of progress.

Operational Infrastructure

We build systems that protect the artist’s attention, income, and direct audience relationships. The tools and boundaries follow the artist’s needs and agreed scope.

Programmatic request gating

Route professional inquiries through a structured form on the artist’s domain, using a tool such as Tally or Typeform. Require project scope, a stated budget that meets the artist’s minimum, and acknowledgment of engagement terms before an inquiry reaches the inbox. Close DMs to professional requests and direct cold contacts to the form through saved replies or email autoresponders.

Single-file edge deployments

Build lightweight sites from portable HTML and CSS, deployed directly to an edge host such as Cloudflare Pages on the artist’s own domain. A simple static site can run without a database, framework, or build pipeline, reducing dependencies and making the work easier to maintain and move between hosts.

Async communication boundaries

Replace open calendar booking with an agreed request process. Require a specific agenda at least 48 hours before a proposed call, including the decision needed and relevant material. Book the meeting once its purpose is clear, protecting the artist’s time for independent work.

Direct payment routing

Use payment links such as Stripe Payment Links connected to the artist’s own account, with payouts to their bank account. Send links directly to buyers or place them on the artist’s site, reducing reliance on marketplace commissions. Payment processing fees and the provider’s data handling still apply; the artist controls the offer and customer relationship.

Custom domain syndicate feeds

Connect a newsletter service such as Buttondown to the artist’s own domain with the required DNS records, alongside an RSS feed on their static site. Build direct subscriber relationships and retain exportable mailing lists and publishing archives, giving audiences ways to follow the work beyond platform algorithms.

Within an Engagement

These practices support the artist’s work within an agreed scope. Participation is voluntary, and the artist can pause, decline, or change direction. Each practice returns to a concrete question: what helps this person sustain the work they want to make?
